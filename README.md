# Insurance Healthcare Analytics Dashboard

## Project Overview

The Insurance Healthcare Analytics Dashboard is an interactive Power BI project designed to analyze medical insurance charges and identify patterns across patient demographics and health-related characteristics.

The analysis covers 1,338 patients and examines medical charges based on age group, gender, smoking status, BMI category, region, and number of children.

## Business Problem

Healthcare and insurance organizations need to understand the factors associated with differences in medical costs.

This project was developed to answer questions such as:

- Which age groups generate the highest medical charges?
- How do charges differ between smokers and non-smokers?
- Is BMI associated with differences in medical charges?
- Which regions have higher average charges?
- How do charges vary across genders?
- How do medical charges vary according to number of children?
- Which patient segments may require additional attention?

## Key KPIs

- Total Patients: 1,338
- Average BMI: 30.66
- Total Smokers: 274
- Average Medical Charges: $13,270.42
- Average Male Age: 39
- Average Female Age: 40

## Key Insights

1. Adults recorded the highest total medical charges at approximately $6.68M.
2. Smokers had significantly higher average medical charges than non-smokers.
3. Average charges were approximately $32,050 for smokers compared with $8,434 for non-smokers.
4. The Obese category recorded the highest total charges among the BMI categories shown.
5. The Southeast recorded the highest average regional charges at approximately $14,735.
6. Medical charges varied across different age groups and numbers of children.
7. The dashboard provides additional analysis of smoking behavior across age groups.
8. Male and female patients had average ages of 39 and 40 respectively.

## Analytical Consideration

The dashboard identifies associations within the dataset but does not establish causation.

For example, the higher average charges observed among smokers indicate an association between smoking status and medical costs, but additional statistical analysis would be required to determine causal relationships.
